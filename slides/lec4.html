AI history

The term Artificial Intelligence is coined in 1956. 

Turing test

The Turing Test is a test for a machine’s ability to exhibit human-like intelligence.

Proposed by Alan Turing in 1950

It involves a human interrogator having text-based conversations with both a human and a machine.

If the interrogator cannot reliably tell the machine from the human, the machine is considered to have passed the test.

The test is designed to assess whether a machine can “think” by successfully imitating human conversation (originally called the “Imitation game”)

Training Neural Networks

Back propagation (1986)

A computer can learn from its mistakes (training)

Backward propagation of errors

An algorithm for supervised learning of artificial neural networks using gradient descent

Created by Geoffrey Hinton

How to Train a Neural Network

  1. Initialize Weights : Start by assigning small, random values to all weights in the network.

  2. Forward Propagation : Feed the network a known input to produce an output or a prediction.

  3. Calculate Loss : Compare the network’s output to the correct output to determine the error.

  4. Backpropagation & Weight Adjustment : Calculate how much each weight contributed to the error, and adjust the weights to reduce this error.

  5. Iterate : Repeat this process many times, allowing the network to gradually improve its accuracy by continuously refining its weights.

Self-Attention

Self-attention allows a model to weigh the importance of different words in a sentence when processing a particular word

  • For example: “The robot picked up the red ball because it was the right color,”self-attention helps the model understand that “it” refers to the “ball” and not the “robot.” 

For each word, the model can “look” at all other words in the input sequence and determine which ones are most relevant to understanding its meaning in that specific context

This ability to capture relationships between words, regardless of their distance from each other in the sentence, is a key advantage over sequential models

Encoder and decoder

Encoder (left): The encoder receives an input and builds a representation of it (its features). This means that the model is optimized to acquire understanding from the input.

Decoder (right): The decoder uses the encoder’s representation (features) along with other inputs to generate a target sequence. This means that the model is optimized for generating outputs.

Tokenization and the Token Window

LLMs don’t use words but  break down text into smaller units called tokens

Tokens are whole words or a part of a word

  • “Amoral” might be 2 tokens “a” and “moral”

The context window is the maximum number of tokens an LLM can “remember” or consider at one time when generating a response

  • “It is the input layer to the neural network”
  • A 100K context windows can approximately contain 75k words

“Thinking” -  From Simple Prediction to Complex Reasoning

A technique where the model is prompted to “think step by step,” breaking down a complex problem into intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer.

This ability to follow a logical chain of thought is significantly more pronounced in larger models.

This is what we use for our AI Agents

LLM  limitations

Hallucinations : LLMs can generate plausible but incorrect or nonsensical information.[23]

Bias : They can reflect and amplify biases present in their training data.

Lack of True Understanding : At their core, LLMs are probabilistic models predicting the next token; they do not possess true consciousness or understanding in the human sense.

Computational Cost : Training and deploying large-scale LLMs requires significant computational resources.

Temperature

Temperature is a key parameter that controls the randomness of an LLM’s output.

It’s a value, typically between 0 and 2, that you can set when you make a request to the model.

Think of it as a dial for creativity.

Low Temperature (e.g., 0.1 to 0.5): With a low temperature, the model becomes more deterministic and conservative.

High Temperature (e.g., 0.8 to 1.5): A higher temperature increases the chances of the model selecting less likely tokens.

More temperature

Not True Creativity : While a higher temperature can lead to more novel-sounding outputs, it’s not the same as human creativity. It’s simply increasing the mathematical randomness of the word selection.[10][15] The model’s inherent creativity is more tied to its architecture and training data.[15]

Risk of Incoherence : Pushing the temperature too high can result in nonsensical or “hallucinated” outputs that don’t make sense.[7]

Finding the Balance : The right temperature setting depends entirely on the task at hand. For a reliable AI agent performing a predictable task, a low temperature is generally preferred.[10] For an agent designed to brainstorm or explore possibilities, a higher temperature might be more appropriate.[9]
